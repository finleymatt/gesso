How chips work

The more people collect an artist, the more their chips are worth.

Every artist on Gesso has their own chip. You earn it by buying their work, it gains value as more people collect them, and you spend it on anything here.

The loop, step by step

From first purchase to your next piece

1

Buy a piece.

Pay by card or Apple Pay. Originals ship crated and insured; prints ship flat. 80% of the price goes to the artist the day it ships.

2

Earn 10% back in their chips.

Chips are priced off the artist's collector count at the moment you buy. Buy early and the same dollars get you more chips. The first 25 collectors get double.

3

Watch the count climb.

Each new person who collects that artist raises the price of every chip already out there, including yours. Buying more from the same artist doesn't move it; only new people do.

4

Spend chips on any work.

At checkout, chips can cover up to half of any piece by any artist. The reserve pays the artist the chip share in cash, so they're paid in full. That purchase earns you a new artist's chips, and the loop starts again.

Milestones

The more you collect, the more you get back

Everything you spend on Gesso counts: originals, prints, objects and project pledges. Milestones are named for the layers of a painting, from the first coat to the finished work.

Paying over time

Collect now, pay in pieces

Every piece can be paid in full, in 4 payments over six weeks, or on layaway over 6 or 10 months. None of them charge interest or run a credit check.

With Pay in 4 the piece ships right away. On layaway, the artist holds it for you and ships it after the last payment. Your chips arrive with each payment.

House rules

Six rules that keep chips honest

One person, one count

Buy five pieces from the same artist and you earn chips five times, but you count as one collector. Objects like mugs and aprons earn chips without counting. The price tracks real collectors, not volume.

Prices only go up

An artist's collector count never shrinks, so their chip price never falls. Early collectors are never punished for showing up first.

Up to half of any piece

Chips can pay for up to 50% of a work. The rest is card or Apple Pay, so real money keeps reaching artists.

Chips are for art first

Spend them on any piece, object, booking or project, and at partner stores. From the Glaze milestone, a weekly drip pays 1% of your chips out to your bank (planned, pending legal review).

They never expire

Hold them for a week or five years. If an artist leaves Gesso, their chips stay spendable on everything else.

Every sale on the record

Each artist page shows the collector count, the chip price history, and provenance for every work, so anyone can check the math.

Questions

Things people ask first

Are chips a cryptocurrency?

Chips are recorded on a public ledger, so anyone can verify how many exist and how they moved. Today they work like store credit that grows with the artist's community. A weekly cash drip and buying chips directly are planned, and both wait on legal review because they turn chips into a financial product.

Which chips get used when I pay?

You choose at checkout. By default Gesso spends the chips with the highest value first, and you can switch any of them off.

What happens if I return a piece?

Returns are open for 14 days after delivery. The chips you earned from that piece are taken back, and any chips you spent on it are returned to you.

How does an artist get paid when I use chips?

The artist gets the full price either way. The card portion comes from you and the chip portion comes from the reserve, which 12% of every sale funds.

Why does a $1,000 piece earn more chips from a new artist?

A new artist's chips are cheap because few people collect them yet, so 10% back buys more chips. If the artist catches on, those chips gain the most.
